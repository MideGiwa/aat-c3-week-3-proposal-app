KOYA TALENT PROPOSALS — STAKEHOLDER DEMO SCRIPT
Total time: 5 minutes (slides ~2:45, live demo ~2:15)
Deck: koya-talent-proposals-demo.html (arrow keys / click sides / bottom dots to navigate)

------------------------------------------------------------
SLIDE 1 — COVER                                    (0:00–0:20)
------------------------------------------------------------
[Just say the name and frame why anyone should care in one breath.]

"This is Koya Talent Proposals — a small internal tool that takes a
proposal from a call to something ready for a client's inbox, with a
human decision at every step that actually matters."

------------------------------------------------------------
SLIDE 2 — THE PROBLEM                              (0:20–0:55)
------------------------------------------------------------
[Advance: press → or click the right side of the screen]

"Right now, after a discovery call, someone on the sales team writes
the proposal by hand — pulling from notes, an old proposal, whatever
template is closest. That's slow, and the result depends entirely on
who happened to write it that week — tone, completeness, all of it
varies. And once it's out for review, there's no shared view of where
it actually stands — you have to ask."

------------------------------------------------------------
SLIDE 3 — THE SOLUTION                             (0:55–1:30)
------------------------------------------------------------
[Advance]

"So the fix isn't 'let AI write the proposal and send it.' It's one
workflow: Claude drafts every section straight from the call intake,
a salesperson reviews and edits that draft instead of starting from
nothing, it goes through internal approval, and it's logged the whole
way through. The salesperson stays the editor — not the author from a
blank page."

------------------------------------------------------------
SLIDE 4 — HOW IT WORKS                             (1:30–2:10)
------------------------------------------------------------
[Advance — this is the six-step flow diagram]

"Concretely, that's six steps: intake, AI draft, review and edit,
approval, send, and logged. Each proposal always has exactly one of
these as its current status, and that status is what the whole team
sees on the dashboard — not something you have to ask about."

[Optional, if you have 10 extra seconds and the room looks technical:]
"And 'review and edit' isn't all-or-nothing — a section can be
regenerated or hand-edited on its own without touching the rest."

------------------------------------------------------------
SLIDE 5 — WHY TEAMS TRUST IT                       (2:10–2:40)
------------------------------------------------------------
[Advance]

"Three things make this safe to actually use. One — if the call notes
are missing something, the draft flags it instead of guessing, and the
proposal can't move to review until that's filled in. Two — nothing
reaches a client without internal approval, and that's enforced on the
server, not just a button we hide in the interface. Three — every
step is logged, so if something fails — a send, a document, anything —
it's visible and debuggable, never silent."

------------------------------------------------------------
SLIDE 6 — TRANSITION TO DEMO                       (2:40–2:55)
------------------------------------------------------------
[Advance — last slide]

"Let's stop talking about it and just watch one go from a call to a
client's inbox."

[Switch over to the running app now.]

------------------------------------------------------------
LIVE DEMO                                          (2:55–5:00, ~2 min)
------------------------------------------------------------
Suggested path through the app — trim to whatever fits the room and
the time you have left. Narrate what status the proposal is in at
each stop; that status is the whole point of the pitch.

  1. Dashboard — show a couple of existing proposals and their
     statuses at a glance (draft / pending review / approved / sent).

  2. Open one proposal (or start a new one from intake) — point out
     the intake details are all there, editable, not thrown away
     after generation.

  3. Show a generated section, and if one has it, a
     [NEEDS INPUT: ...] marker — this is the "don't guess" behavior
     from slide 5, made concrete.

  4. Regenerate or hand-edit one section — show the rest of the
     proposal is untouched.

  5. Submit for approval → show the approval screen / decision.
     (If you have an approver login handy, make the decision live.)

  6. Send (or show a proposal already marked "sent") → open the
     generated PDF briefly.

  7. Close on the dashboard again, now showing that proposal as
     "sent" — the loop closes exactly where slide 6 said it would.

CLOSING LINE (if you have 10 seconds left):
"That's the whole loop — draft, review, approve, send, logged —
happy to take questions or go deeper on any piece of it."
